What these modules are

Most of Taskiem is a workflow engine. These packages are the products built on it for small Nigerian businesses: a record of the money that moves on their rails, invoices and the matching of money to them, a school's fees, a one-page storefront, hosted forms, and the data tables and files that hold the business's own records. Each is a platform record: its tables live in Taskiem's Postgres under forced row-level security, people change it through /v1, and workflows reach it through a platform connector that needs no credentials (invoices@1, schools@1, storefront@1, form@1). None of them runs inside the engine. They start runs through the ordinary runtime.Start, and they are started by loops in the api role.

The money log

The money log is one row per movement of money on the business's connected rails, and one per later status of it, written once and never changed (RecordTx). What makes a row one movement is its key: the channel, the event and the rail's reference, and for a credit to a customer's permanent account the rail's credit_id as well, because one account takes many credits (Entry.Key). An account credit that comes without a credit_id is not recorded at all, since keyed by the account alone it would hide the account's next credit; the owners are told, once per connection a day, to check it on the rail and enter it by hand if the look-back does not bring it (AlertCreditWithoutID). A unique index on the key makes a payment heard by webhook, by the poller, by a payment step's answer and by the look-back one row. A later status (a payout reversed, a payment returned) is a new row naming the first in corrects, and the reports read each movement's latest row.

Amounts are kobo in bigint, never floats; a rail's naira amount is rounded to kobo in its manifest (int(math.round(double(...) * 100.0))). The payer's name and account are sealed before the row is written. Rows also come from people: cash and expenses entered by hand (Enter), and bank statements uploaded as CSV, each line keyed by channel, date, amount and reference. A background job copies every row to the business's kept data table money_log in Xenibase, where people read, search and export it but cannot change it.

One movement of money in can count towards several invoices and orders, a split. Each part is a payment row (customer_invoice_payments or storefront_order_payments), undone by another row, never changed. Both kinds of target count and undo parts through one path, under an advisory lock on the entry (EntryLockSQL, Parts.Count), and a trigger in migration 00210 refuses a part that would make the parts add up to more than the entry counts for, whoever writes it. What an entry counts for is what the payer sent: the amount, plus the rail's fee when the rail settled net.

Matching

Every money-in entry is looked at once (consider), in this order:

  1. Exact, by reference. A payment into a one-time account number or link Taskiem gave for an invoice or an order counts towards it with nobody asked.
  2. Exact, by account. A credit to a customer's permanent account counts towards that customer's open invoices oldest first, then their siblings' where the school shares family credit; what is left is the customer's credit (CountCustomer). A credit whose account number and reference disagree is held for a person.
  3. Exact, by checkout. A payment through a storefront order's Paystack page counts towards the order.
  4. Suggested. Otherwise the 500 most recently sent open invoices and the open orders are scored from 0 to 100 on amount, payer name, reference, date and payers remembered from earlier matches. Up to three scoring 40 or more are offered, each with its reasons. When the business turns automatic matching on, a top score of 85 or more that is at least 15 above the next is matched on its own.

The three exact rules are skipped for money a person entered (cash entered by hand, or an uploaded statement line) when the business runs a school with a cash threshold: such money is only suggested, so a person matches it (exact). How that match can wait for a second person is under Schools.

A person confirms or rejects a suggestion, or matches an entry by hand in one or more parts (count). The loop runs every 10 seconds in every api replica; a unique index on suggestions and the entry lock make two replicas looking at the same entry harmless. Money that went back to the payer (a returned payment, a reversed POS sale, a refund that settled) undoes the invoice parts it counted, reopens the invoices, voids their school receipts and tells the owners (reverse). Parts counted towards orders are left, as orders have their own refunds.

Invoices and receiving money

An invoice's status and what was paid are computed by the database from when it was sent or voided, its due date and its payment rows (migration 00197): nothing marks an invoice paid but a payment counted against it (CountPayment). The same payment id counts towards an invoice once while it is not undone, under a second advisory lock on the invoice and payment id. A sent invoice changes only by an amendment with a reason: an added line, a discount, or an item taken out.

An invoice's page is /i/{token} on the form host, a 24-byte token stored only as a hash. There the customer can ask for a one-time account number or a payment link. The public role only queues the request (taskiem_public_invoice_request, at most 5 an hour and 20 in all per invoice); a one-second loop in the api role issues it on the business's rail (Drain), and a 30-second loop asks the rail whether it was paid (Received). Rails that cannot be asked, such as iswallet, are heard by their webhook alone. A payment counted queues the runs of every prod workflow whose trigger is {"type": "invoice", "config": {"event": "paid"}} or payment, in the payment's own transaction, once per invoice (or part) and workflow (queuePaidStarts).

Receive money with Taskiem (receive_money.go) gives a business without a rail of its own a wallet under Taskiem's iswallet client, as the connection iswallet/taskiem, so everything above works on it.

Money reports and the digest

moneyreport sums a period from each movement's latest entry: money in and out, settled and pending, by channel, the fees and the effective fee rate, the invoices paid and the leaks. It exports the money log, the invoices and an accounting journal as CSV, with text cells guarded against formula injection. moneydigest sends yesterday's summary each morning from 08:00 Lagos on WhatsApp or Telegram, and last month's on the 1st by email. Each summary is claimed once per business, kind and period in money_digests, in the transaction that queues it, so restarts and several schedulers never send one twice.

Leaks

Every 15 minutes Detector.Tenant runs seven queries per business: money in unmatched after a day, overdue invoices, the same reference and amount twice on a channel, an invoice paid more than it asked, money paid for a voided invoice, a fee above the rate agreed with a connection, and cash a rider owes beyond the limit. A leak opens once per kind and subject, is closed by a person (recovered, written off, not a leak), and closes itself when its cause goes away.

The duplicate query groups money by channel, direction, reference and amount. For a credit to a customer's permanent account it uses the rail's credit_id in place of the reference, because the reference is the account's and one account takes many equal payments, such as a term's fees paid in two halves (the query).

Resolved

The duplicate query used to group account credits by the account's reference, so two equal payments into one student's account opened a "duplicate" leak for money that was not duplicated. This was found while writing this page.

The look-back

For every prod connection that issued customers' account numbers and maps list_credits, every 15 minutes, Look reads the last 48 hours of credits back and records each through the money log with the webhook's key, so a credit heard both ways is one entry. A credit the webhook should have brought and did not, more than 30 minutes old, tells the owners once a day that the webhook may not be set up; three failures in a row raise an alert. Who reads a connection is claimed in the database, so two api replicas do not both read it.

When a read says it was truncated, the window is read again in quarters. If a quarter is still truncated, what was read is recorded, but the run counts as a failure, and three in a row raise the alert (read). list_credits has no pages, so the credits beyond the rail's limit in that quarter are still not read back; the alert is how a person hears of it.

Resolved

A quarter that was still truncated used to be only logged, and the run was recorded as successful, so nobody was told that credits were left unread. This was found while writing this page.

Schools

The Schools area (decision 0046) is a school's fees on top of invoices: classes, sessions and terms, students, families and guardians, fee items per class and term, discounts, a term's invoices made once per student and term, account numbers for each student, cash posted at the desk, receipts with a QR check at /r/{token}, a family's page at /s/{token}, reminders and promises, payment plans, clearance at the gate and a daily close. The database keeps its rules (migration 00211): unique admission numbers, one current term, past terms read-only. Students' and guardians' names, dates of birth and phones are sealed under their own subjects, so erasing a person erases them, and kept copies go to the data tables school_students, school_classes and school_term_fees.

Money reaches a student's fees by one path, the permanent-account rule above. Cash, transfers and POS payments a bursar posts are written as a money log entry (posting:<id>) and counted the same way (PostPayment). The person who asked for anything below can never decide it, with no exception for a one-person school. A second person with school.approve decides:

  • Cash at the desk at or above the school's cash threshold. A posting under it still waits when, added to what money people entered already counts towards the student's open invoices without a second person, it reaches the threshold, so postings split under it add up.
  • Money entered on Money and matched by hand. Cash entered at POST /v1/money/entries (or in chat) and uploaded statement lines are the same money. Matching one to an invoice when it is at or above the cash threshold, or when it brings the person-entered money on that invoice without approval to the threshold, records a cash_match request in school_pending_postings and counts nothing (holdMatch, EnteredUnapproved). The API answers 202 needs_approval (matchHeld); approval makes the match as it was asked for (ApproveHeld). Automatic matching never makes such a match. Money a rail reported (its events, the look-back, a step's answer) is never held.
  • Discounts and amendments that lower an invoice. A standing discount at or above the discount threshold waits. So does an amendment to a sent invoice that lowers its total, a discount or an item taken out, weighed by what it takes off together with what earlier amendments took off the same invoice without a second person (RequestDiscountAmendment, ReducedUnapproved). The API answers 202 needs_approval (amendInvoice).
Resolved

The cash threshold used to be checked only in PostPayment, so anyone with invoice.manage could enter cash on Money and match it by hand to a student's invoice with no second person, even with the threshold at 0. Taking an item out of a sent invoice was not weighed against the discount threshold at all, and both thresholds were per request, so several requests just below one passed. All three were found while writing this page.

Risk

The totals that stop splitting are per invoice (for a desk posting, the student's open invoices), and a standing discount is still weighed per request. Payments split across a student's invoices, or several standing discounts just below the threshold, pass without a second person. There is no total per student or per day.

The hold looks at whether the business runs a school, not whether the invoice is a school's: in a business with Schools on, a hand match of entered money to any of its invoices at or above the cash threshold waits for a second person (CashLimit). That errs towards holding, but the code's own comment speaks of the school's invoices, so a shop's invoice in the same business waits too.

Storefronts

A storefront is a business's one-page public site (decision 0036): a card the owner edits, a catalogue mapped from a data table, actions (order, enquiry, callback, booking), and publishing a snapshot the public host serves. The catalogue's public columns are copied into storefront_catalogue, so page views never reach Xenibase. An order's journey:

  1. The public host checks the form (a signed token, a honeypot, a minimum fill time, proof of work, a rate limit) and writes it, encrypted under a key the api role shares, to storefront_inbox. It cannot seal personal data itself: it has no vault.
  2. The api role opens it, seals the customer, checks prices and stock against the catalogue table, and records storefront_orders. A price that changed holds the order and tells the customer.
  3. An order paid at once gets a Paystack page, checked every 30 seconds and counted when paid at least the total (markPaid), through the same money log entry and parts as matching. Pay by bank transfer on the status page issues a one-time account number as invoices do.
  4. The action's workflow starts with the storefront trigger, deduplicated on the order's id, within the plan's limits (startRun). storefront@1 lets it accept, decline, complete or cancel, ask for payment, notify the customer and refund.

A business can bring its own domain and prove it with a TXT record at _taskiem-verify.<domain>. The first business to prove a domain serves it; another takes it over only once it is unverified or removed, and every domain is proven again every few hours (VerifyDomain).

Spec and code differ
  • The spec keeps orders and bookings as rows of orders and bookings data tables in Xenibase (spec 8.1). Both are rows of storefront_orders in Taskiem's database (a booking is an order of kind booking, its slot held in storefront_holds), which is what lets them take part in matching and the money log.
  • Spec 7.5 has submission limits per page and per hour with alerts, per phone number on orders, and 100 a day per IP (spec 7.5). The code has a bucket per IP hash only, 20 every 10 minutes (SubmissionsPerIP), and the plan's run limits behind it. formguard's comment says per-page and per-phone limits "sit on top"; none exist. The optional phone code on orders is not built.

The public host and its isolation

publicweb serves storefronts, hosted forms, invoice pages, receipts and family pages to anyone. It runs as the public role, apart from the API and the edge, with its own database login and a pool of five connections (TASKIEM_PUBLIC_DATABASE_URL). That login is taskiem_public, which reads no table at all: it may only execute SECURITY DEFINER functions (38 are defined across the migrations) named taskiem_public_*, owned by taskiem_dispatch, each of which takes a host, a token's hash or a reference with its key and answers only what a page shows.

Isolation between businesses rests on three things, and each held where it was checked:

  • The host decides the tenant. A request's host is looked up once (DBLookup, cached 30 seconds, misses 10), and every storefront page then reads by that site's id. Status pages, payment, rating and change pages read an order by reference and its status key, and refuse one whose storefront is not the host's (statusOf), so a reference from another shop answers "not found".
  • Tokens, not ids, on the form host. Invoice, receipt, family and form-link pages are reached by 24-byte random tokens stored as hashes; a malformed, unknown and expired token answer the same page. Each function also checks the tenant is active.
  • Writes are requests. The public role never writes a tenant's record. It queues: an order in storefront_inbox, a submission in form_inbox, an account-number request, a family's "I have paid" or plan request in school_family_inbox, each with its own limits counted in the database (taskiem_public_school_family_ask: 5 claims an hour, 3 plan requests, 3 new links), and the api role does the rest.

Every response carries the public security headers, with frames allowed only under /embed/ and only from the business's verified websites; any cookie a handler tries to set is dropped and logged (cookieless). Page views are limited to 300 a minute per address, form submissions to 20 every 10 minutes on storefronts and 50 an hour on the form host, and "Report this page" separately. TLS certificates for businesses' domains are issued only for hosts that serve (TLSAsk).

Spec and code differ

The spec has cache misses "read the read replica, not the primary" (spec 8.4). The public host connects to TASKIEM_PUBLIC_DATABASE_URL, which defaults to the primary; a replica is the operator's choice.

Forms

A hosted form (decision 0037) is either a workflow's trigger, {"type": "form"} on forms.<domain>/f/{id}, or a form step that waits for a person: a member in the app, or an outsider through a one-time link (/r/{token}) with an optional code by SMS or email. The form host checks each page and seals what it receives for the api role; Process checks it again against the deployed version's schema, keeps files in the business's private Xenibase bucket, seals the fields the schema marks personal and anything that looks personal, records form_submissions once per reference, and starts the run or answers the step. Files are read back only through signed URLs, by a person with pii.reveal or a form@1 step.

Spec and code differ

The spec limits submissions "per form and per IP hash" (spec). There is a limit per IP hash only (FormSubmissionsPerIP), and the organisation's ingest limits.

A member's list of form steps waiting for their roles is filtered by those roles in the query, owners seeing every role's, newest 100 first (listFormRequests).

Resolved

The list used to read the 100 newest pending requests of any role and filter by role afterwards, so in a business with more than 100 waiting for other roles a member saw none of theirs. This was found while writing this page.

Data tables, on Xenibase

Data tables (decision 0033) are a business's own records, such as customers, stock and staff, kept as Postgres tables in a Xenibase project, one project per organisation. Taskiem keeps the definitions (data_tables, data_table_columns) and the project's state; the rows live only in Xenibase. The first table provisions the project; schema changes go through Xenibase's preview and apply, never SQL text Taskiem writes; and the connection's token is renewed before it expires. Workflows reach the rows through the ordinary xenibase@1 connection named data_tables in prod, and the API through the same connector's actions, so this package never sees a row.

Some tables are kept by Taskiem: money_log, money_matches, school_students, school_classes, school_term_fees. Their rows are copies only Taskiem's own writers add. The page, the rows API, chat and the storefront refuse to change them, and the worker refuses a write step that names one, or any call_function on the project while it keeps a table (GuardStep). A business that brought its own project can still edit them in Xenibase; since the records are copies, that changes no money.

Media

media stores a business's files in its Xenibase project's storage, in two buckets. The public one holds storefront photos: decoded only after the header says at most 25 million pixels, turned upright, stripped of metadata, stored at fixed widths under content-named paths and cached for good. The private one holds form uploads (JPEG, PNG and PDF up to 25 MB), reached only through signed URLs that last at most seven days. Taskiem uses the project's API token through Xenibase's management API, so no storage policy is needed and none can open them.

How tightly each sits on the engine

The modules are loosely coupled to the runtime and tightly coupled to each other. None is in the engine's packages, none changes how a run executes, and each reaches runs only by runtime.Start, a signal to a waiting step, or a platform connector. Their loops all run in the api role (runInvoicePayments: requests every second, payments every 30 seconds, matching every 10, leaks every 15 minutes; the look-back every minute). Between them the coupling is deliberate: Schools is invoices plus records; invoices, orders, cash postings and the look-back all count money through the money log's one path; and the public host only reads published pages and writes requests the api role picks up.
