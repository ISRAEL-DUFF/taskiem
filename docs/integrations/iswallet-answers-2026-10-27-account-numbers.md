# iswallet — generating account numbers for your customers

**For:** the Taskiem engineering team
**Date:** 2026-10-27
**Scope:** creating a wallet for one of your customers and giving them a bank account
number they can pay into. Payouts, FX and crypto are out of scope here.

---

## 1. What you will build

```
POST /v1/wallets                                     → a wallet
POST /v1/wallets/{wallet_id}/virtual-account         → an account number to pay into
POST /v1/wallets/{wallet_id}/virtual-accounts/batch  → …or up to 500 at once
POST /v1/webhooks/subscriptions                      → once, for your whole integration
```

Nothing is auto-created. A wallet starts with **no** account number anyone can pay into;
the second call is what produces one.

**For your schools model that is one wallet per school and one account per student**, all
on that wallet — see [§5](#5-one-wallet-many-accounts--the-school-model). Read §3 and §4
first for the single-account case; §5 is the shape you will actually build.

## 2. Before you start, from us

We create your client record and issue your API key. You will receive:

- a **client key** — send it as `Authorization: Bearer <key>`
- your **`client_id`**
- the **base URL** for sandbox and for production

Your key reaches only your own wallets. Keep it server-side; it must never reach a browser
or a mobile app.

## 3. Create a wallet

```bash
curl -X POST "$BASE/v1/wallets" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $KEY" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: $(uuidgen)" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "owner_type": "client_end_user",
    "owner_ref": "taskiem:user:88213",
    "currency": "NGN",
    "email": "ada@example.com"
  }'
```

- **`Idempotency-Key` is required.** Without it: `400 MISSING_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY`. Generate a
  fresh UUID per customer and reuse it if you retry, so a retry cannot create a second
  wallet.
- **`owner_ref` is your own id for the customer.** Use something you can look up — it is
  how you will find the wallet again. One wallet per `(owner_ref, currency)`.
- **Identity fields are flat.** `email`, `bvn`, `first_name` and the rest go at the top
  level. Do **not** wrap them in a `kyc_profile` object — a body containing one is
  rejected. (An earlier version of our documentation described that object. It was wrong,
  and it cost another integrator a failed import, so the API now refuses it outright
  rather than creating a wallet with no identity.)

### Choosing `owner_type`

| Your customer is… | Use |
|---|---|
| An individual | `client_end_user` |
| A business holding **other people's** money — a school taking fees, an employer funding payroll | `client_end_user_business` |
| A business holding **only its own** money | `client_end_business` |

The two business types differ in **where the money is held**: segregated in that
business's own account at our bank partner, or pooled with ours and tracked on our ledger.
**This is not trivially reversible**, so please settle it with us before you onboard at
volume. If all your customers are individuals, use `client_end_user` and move on.

### ⚠️ The `account_number` in this response is NOT a bank account

```json
{
  "wallet_id": "5b00d9dd-…",
  "account_number": "6256638601",
  "internal_reference": "6256638601",
  "tier": "TIER_1"
}
```

`account_number` here is **our own internal handle for the wallet**. It is generated by us,
no bank is involved, and **no one can transfer money to it.** If you show it to a customer
as a deposit account, every transfer will be refused at the sending bank — and because the
money never reaches us, there will be no failed payment on our side for anyone to find.

Read **`internal_reference`** instead. It is the same value under a name that cannot be
misread. `account_number` exists only because removing it would break integrators already
decoding this response, and it is deprecated.

**The number your customer pays into comes from step 4.**

## 4. Issue the account number

### A reusable account — what you probably want

One per customer, kept forever, every transfer to it credits the same wallet:

```bash
curl -X POST "$BASE/v1/wallets/$WALLET_ID/virtual-account" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "type": "permanent",
    "reference": "taskiem:user:88213",
    "account_name": "Ada Obi"
  }'
```

```json
{
  "account_number": "0107062314",
  "bank_name": "…",
  "account_name": "Ada Obi",
  "is_permanent": true,
  "reference": "taskiem:user:88213",
  "status": "active"
}
```

**This `account_number` is the one to show your customer.**

- **`reference` is yours** and it makes this call idempotent: ask twice with the same
  reference and you get the same account back, never a second one. Retries are free.
- **`account_name`** is what a payer sees when their banking app resolves the account
  before they confirm. Use the customer's name.
- No `Idempotency-Key` needed — `reference` already does that job here.

### A one-time account — for a single expected payment

If you want an account tied to one payment of a known amount (a task fee, a checkout):

```bash
  -d '{"type":"dynamic","amount":5000000,"reference":"task:TSK-4417"}'
```

- **`amount` is in kobo** — `5000000` is ₦50,000 — and it is **required**.
- **These are NOT idempotent.** Every call creates another account, including a retry. If a
  call fails ambiguously, assume you may now hold two accounts for one task.
- They carry a 30-minute `expires_at`, but **treat that as advisory**: we do not refuse a
  late payment, and we do not refuse a second one. If you need a hard cutoff, enforce it
  on your side when the credit arrives and refund if it is too late.
- **Still send a `reference`** — it is how the payment resolves back to the task.

Use reusable accounts unless you specifically need the amount locked. They are simpler,
idempotent, and your customer keeps one number.

### What must be set before this call succeeds

| Requirement | Applies to | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| `email` (or `business_email`) | every wallet | `422 KYC_REQUIRED` |
| A director's **BVN** | the two business types | `422 KYC_REQUIRED` |

An individual with just an email is enough. The message names the missing field.

### Choosing the bank partner (optional)

```
GET /v1/providers
```

```json
{ "providers": [
  { "code": "flutterwave", "currencies": ["NGN"],
    "virtual_accounts": { "permanent": true, "dynamic": true } }
]}
```

Pass `"provider": "<code>"` on issuance to pick one. **Omit it and we choose** — that is
the right default unless you have a reason. A wallet may hold accounts at more than one
partner, and money sent to any of them lands in the same wallet.

## 5. One wallet, many accounts — the school model

This is the shape you described: **one wallet for the school**, and **one account number
per student**, so every student's payment lands in the school's balance.

That is exactly what this is built for. Issue one permanent account per student on the
same wallet, each with its own `reference`:

```bash
POST /v1/wallets/$SCHOOL_WALLET_ID/virtual-account
{ "type":"permanent", "reference":"student:gca-2026-0417", "account_name":"Ada Obi" }
```

Every one is a distinct NUBAN. Every credit carries its `reference` back to you, so a
payment resolves to a student without a lookup table of your own that can drift. There is
no practical cap on accounts per wallet.

> Earlier this was not safe: a second permanent account on the same wallet silently
> **overwrote** the first and returned `200`, so an integrator issuing one per student
> would have ended up with one account and no error. It is keyed on `reference` precisely
> so that cannot happen. Always send one.

### Issuing a roll in bulk

```bash
POST /v1/wallets/$SCHOOL_WALLET_ID/virtual-accounts/batch
{ "accounts": [ { "reference": "student:gca-2026-0417", "account_name": "Ada Obi" },
                { "reference": "student:gca-2026-0418", "account_name": "Bayo Ade" } ] }
```

- **500 accounts per call**, issued 8 at a time
- **per-item results** — `created` / `existing` / `failed`, each with its own error code. A
  batch never collapses into "397 of 400 succeeded", which you could not act on
- **idempotent per reference**, so re-running after a partial failure is safe
- **permanent accounts only**, which matches the recommendation above
- one partner per batch, so a partial failure cannot split an import across partners

Use it for an enrolment sitting; use single calls for a student added mid-term.

### Where the money sits, if the wallet is a business wallet

Relevant only to `client_end_user_business` — the type whose balance is held in the
business's own segregated account at our bank partner. You can skip this if your schools
are `client_end_user`.

A per-student account is **not** bound to that segregated account. The partner gives a
payout sub-account exactly **one** static number, so only a wallet's single *unreferenced*
account can be bound to it. Per-student accounts therefore settle through our pool, and a
sweep moves the money into the school's segregated account afterwards.

What this means in practice:

- **the school's balance is correct immediately.** The ledger credits on the deposit, and
  nothing you or the school can see is delayed — balance, transaction history, the credit
  event
- **the physical location of the money lags** by roughly the time it takes us to sweep.
  That lag is bounded, monitored and self-correcting, with a default alarm at 10 minutes

So segregation for per-student accounts is **eventual, not instantaneous**. It does not
change what you build, and it does not change what the school sees — but it is a real
property of the design, and you should hear it from us rather than discover it.

If a school ever needs payments to land in its segregated account directly, that is the
wallet's single unreferenced account — one number for the whole school, and no per-student
attribution from the number. That is the trade; for your use case, per-student is right.

## 6. Find out when money arrives

Subscribe **once** for your whole integration:

```bash
curl -X POST "$BASE/v1/webhooks/subscriptions" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $KEY" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: $(uuidgen)" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "endpoint_url": "https://taskiem.example/hooks/iswallet",
    "event_types": ["wallet.credit.posted"]
  }'
```

The **`signing_secret` is returned once, at creation.** Store it then; there is no way to
read it back.

A credit arrives as:

```json
{
  "event_type": "wallet.credit.posted",
  "wallet_id": "5b00d9dd-…",
  "data": {
    "amount": 5000000,
    "amount_credited": 4998750,
    "currency": "NGN",
    "virtual_account": {
      "reference": "taskiem:user:88213",
      "account_number": "0107062314",
      "status": "active"
    }
  }
}
```

Three things to get right:

1. **Match on `virtual_account.reference`** — your own id. That is what resolves a payment
   to a customer or a task.
2. **`amount` vs `amount_credited`.** `amount` is what the payer sent; `amount_credited` is
   what landed in the wallet after our partner's fee. They differ whenever the fee is
   passed to the customer, which is the default. **Credit your customer with
   `amount_credited`**; show `amount` on a receipt.
3. **Verify the signature.** `X-iSpend-Signature: sha256=<hex>` is
   `HMAC-SHA256(secret, timestamp + "." + rawBody)` — note the literal `.`, and use the
   **raw** body before any JSON reparse. Reject if `X-iSpend-Timestamp` is more than 300
   seconds from now.

Event names must match **exactly**; there are no wildcards. A missing name in
`event_types` is the most common reason for "we never got the webhook".

Retries: up to 10 attempts with exponential backoff. One event produces one delivery, so
duplicates at your end are retries — **be idempotent on `X-iSpend-Idempotency-Key`**.

Troubleshooting without asking us:
`GET /v1/webhooks/subscriptions/{id}/deliveries` shows whether we sent it and what your
endpoint returned.

## 7. When something fails

| Status | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| `400 MISSING_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY` | No `Idempotency-Key` on wallet creation | Add one |
| `400 INVALID_REQUEST` | Bad body — e.g. a nested `kyc_profile`, or a dynamic account with no `amount` | Fix and resend |
| `422 KYC_REQUIRED` | A prerequisite is missing; the message names the field | Supply it, resend |
| `422 PROVIDER_NOT_CONFIGURED` | That partner cannot issue that kind of account for that currency | Check `GET /v1/providers` |
| `502 PROVIDER_REJECTED` | The bank partner refused. **Nothing was created.** The partner's own reason is in the message | Do **not** retry unchanged. Fix what it names, or tell us |
| `500 INTERNAL_ERROR` | Ours, and ambiguous — the account may or may not exist | Send us the `request_id`; do not loop |

**Every response carries `X-Request-ID`. Please log it.** When you report a problem, that
one string takes us straight to the request. Without it we are matching on timestamps.

The difference between the last two rows matters: `502` is final and safe to treat as
such; `500` means we need to look.

## 8. Suggested order of work

1. `GET /v1/providers` with your sandbox key — confirms auth works, costs nothing.
2. Create one wallet. Confirm you are storing `wallet_id` and **not** treating
   `account_number` as payable.
3. Issue one reusable account. Confirm the number you show customers comes from **this**
   response.
4. Subscribe your webhook, verify a signature end to end, and make your handler idempotent.
5. Credit your customer from `amount_credited`.
6. Issue a roll through the batch endpoint (§5) once single issuance works.

You do not need one-time accounts for this. Permanent accounts keyed on your `reference`
give you per-student attribution, free retries and no duplicate-account risk; the batch
endpoint issues permanent accounts only. Skip the one-time path entirely — §4 describes it
for completeness, not as something you need.

## Addendum, later the same day: the webhook endpoint

> Kept verbatim. iswallet's answer to "what is the webhook URL".

> There's no iswallet URL to point at — the webhook endpoint is yours, hosted on your side, and you give it to us when you subscribe via the `endpoint_url` field on `POST /v1/webhooks/subscriptions` (you can change it later with a `PATCH` to the same subscription). The path is entirely your choice, e.g. `https://taskiem.example/hooks/iswallet`, and you'll want a separate subscription per environment since each one gets its own signing secret, returned only once at creation. It must be **https** and publicly resolvable — URLs that resolve to private or loopback addresses are refused with `400 INVALID_ENDPOINT_URL`. Your endpoint should **return a 2xx within 30 seconds** and do the real work afterwards; anything else, including a 4xx, counts as a failure and is retried up to 10 times with backoff before we dead-letter it. Two things to build in from the start: verify the `X-iSpend-Signature` header before trusting the body, and make the handler idempotent on `X-iSpend-Idempotency-Key`, since retries are normal. If deliveries ever look wrong at your end, `GET /v1/webhooks/subscriptions/{id}/deliveries` shows you our status code and your response body for each attempt.
